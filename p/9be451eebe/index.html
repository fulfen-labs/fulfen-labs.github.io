Every demo works. Little of it ships.

The gap between a pilot and production is rarely the model. It is the system around it: grounding in the organisation's own data, contracts with its systems, evaluations, security, release gates and someone who owns the result.

That system is the real work, and it should belong to the organisation that runs it. The handbook shows how to build it with the people who will use it, inside their own systems, and how to hand it over so they can run and improve it alone.

The method is in print

The Forward Deployed Engineer's Handbook sets it out in 27 chapters and six parts, from the role itself to handover. Each chapter has a video lesson, presented by AI characters; lesson 1 is public now, and the rest are listed here as they are published.

About the handbookWatch lesson 1

What the method holds to

Five principles behind the method.

Built to leave

Success is what the receiving team can do after the builders leave. A permanent dependency counts as a failure.

Verified value, not output

Generated output is inventory until it is checked. Work is paced to how fast it can be checked, and judged by evidence against the outcome agreed at the start.

Prompts suggest; systems enforce

Rules that must always hold live in code, permissions, schemas and release gates a security team can inspect, not in instructions an agent may ignore.

Your estate, your vendors

Build on the cloud, models, identity and governance the organisation already has, and leave nothing that depends on the builder's. The handbook names families of technology, not products, so its advice holds when products change.

Honest about "not yet"

Stopping is a valid result. Sometimes the answer is "not yet", or "this should not use AI", and saying so in week one is much cheaper than in month three.

Twenty-two working rules

The handbook's field judgements, each set against the alternative it beats, rest on five ideas: put what must hold where it cannot be argued with; assume the model will sometimes be wrong or fooled; spend scarce resources deliberately; earn autonomy and change on evidence; keep the system explicable and changeable.
