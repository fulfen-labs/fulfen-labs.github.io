From pilot to a system your team owns.

Engagements follow the arc of the handbook. Each stage ends with evidence you can inspect and a decision: continue, change course, pause or stop. Stopping is a good result when the evidence says so.

Every engagement is led by Daniel Vaughan, who directs the coding agents that do much of the building and signs off what they produce.

Four stages

Take them in order, or start where you are.

Production-readiness review

For
A pilot that works in a demo and has not reached production.
What happens
A short, fixed review against what production needs: grounding and output contracts, integration, evaluation, security and access for agents, governance, observability, cost and ownership.
You get
A written assessment, the gaps ranked by risk, and a recommended route. Sometimes that is an honest "not yet", or "this should not use AI".
Handbook
Chapters 9, 18 to 22

Discovery and design

For
An outcome you want from AI, before anything is built.
What happens
Discovery where the work happens: the work itself, what people know, and the systems, data and tools. Then a design your team, your security team and an independent reviewer can each challenge and accept.
You get
The findings, an outcome with its measures, a defensible design, and an evaluation plan agreed before the build.
Handbook
Part II, chapters 5 to 9

Build to production

For
A designed use case that needs to run for real.
What happens
The system built inside your estate, on your cloud and your choice of models, with evaluations and release gates from the first day. Prototype code is thrown away; the specification it proved is kept.
You get
A running system in production behind release gates, with its observability, cost view and audit trail.
Handbook
Parts III to V, chapters 10 to 22

Prove and hand over

For
A system your own team must run and change.
What happens
Handover planned from the start and passed against milestones: your team watches, assists, runs the system with us on hand, runs it alone through an incident, then ships an improvement through the gates without us.
You get
A team that owns the system, runbooks, the evaluation suite as a living contract, and the value measured against the outcome agreed at the start.
Handbook
Chapters 22, 25 and 26

What we hold to

Built to leave

The engagement is finished when your team can run and improve the system without us. A permanent dependency counts as a failure.

Your estate, your vendors

We build on your cloud, with your choice of models, under your identity and governance, and leave nothing that depends on ours.

Prompts suggest; systems enforce

Guarantees live in code, schemas, tests and release gates that your security team can inspect, not in instructions an agent may ignore.

Verified value, not output

Generated code is not progress. Work is paced to how fast it can be evaluated and judged by evidence against the outcome you set.
