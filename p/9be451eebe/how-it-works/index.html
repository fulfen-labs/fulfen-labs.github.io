The handbook is the method.

The Forward Deployed Engineer's Handbook sets out how an AI system gets from pilot to production in six parts, from the role itself to the human work that decides whether a system is used.

The book names families of technology rather than products, so its advice holds when the products change.

Part 01

The Role

What a forward deployed engineer is: embedded with the customer, writing production code, owning the outcome and carrying what the field teaches back into the practice.

Part 02

The Method

From an ambiguous request to a design that the customer, their security team and an independent reviewer can each challenge and accept, before anything is built.

Part 03

Ground

Models, context and knowledge: the foundation every agent stands on, chosen and tested against the customer's own data and constraints.

Part 04

Orchestrate

Tools, protocols, agents and people working together, and the second job: changing how the customer builds software, so the change outlasts the engagement.

Part 05

Prove

Evidence that the system works, stays safe and can be run by someone else: evaluations, release gates, governance and a clean handover.

Part 06

The Human Side

Trust, sponsors, value, adoption and the conversations that decide whether a working system is actually used.

Understand, design, ground, orchestrate, prove, transfer.

The handbook's lifecycle arc. It is a teaching sequence, not a waterfall: real work loops through it one slice at a time, and the next slice starts while the first is in production. Each stage ends with evidence and a decision: continue, change course, pause or stop.

Ready for production means six things

Security, reliability, observability, governance, operations, cost and performance. A demo runs on curated data with cooperative users; production needs all six to hold at once.
